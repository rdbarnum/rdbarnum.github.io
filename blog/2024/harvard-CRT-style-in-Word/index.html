Hi all,

I wanted to share one utility in word that I have found helpful in the past when writing technical papers and may be useful in this course. The citations & bibliography utility is built into most desktop versions of MS Word since about 2007. Through OU we have access to Microsoft 365, often used online, but the desktop version is also accessible through the 365 subscription. Screenshots in this brief tutorial will be a mix of Word 2010 Professional which I own from my time as an undergraduate, and the current version of Word from Microsoft 365 (Word 2024). The utility remains basically unchanged in practice since 2007, I merely installed office 365 desktop to confirm the utility was still available (as it is not available in the online version from what I can tell) and to provide before and after screenshots.

Reference styles and source management can be found under References -> Citations & Bibliography in the ribbon. In a clean installation, the utility has a number of citation styles as seen in the next figure. Among them is a Harvard citation style developed by Anglia Ruskin University (ARU). Note there are several slightly differing variations of the Harvard style (name, year), of which the ARU style is just one.

An open source repository of additional styles for this Word utility called BibWord was developed to expand the number of citation styles available. The original repository is no longer accessible, but happily the .xml files and instructions for their installation (for whatever version of word you have, even legacy) have been preserved on GitHub here: https://github.com/codingo/BibWord. BibWord contains several Harvard styles, including another ARU version, as well as AGPS, Exeter and Leeds. A CTR Harvard style is also available, developed by University of the Highlands and Islands (UHI) by modifying one of the BibWord versions. It is available for download here: https://uhi.libanswers.com/faq/269538. When you install all the BibWord styles, including the CTR version, your reference styles list will look like the screenshot below. Note that it is only necessary to install the extra style(s) you need or desire, but you may install all of them if you wish.

I hope you find this brief guide useful. If you have any questions about using the Citations & Bibliography utility in Word, I will help if I am able; please post questions. I have found this utility extremely useful for citation management when writing papers in the past and I hope you will as well.

Best,

Bob
